📖 Grammar: Maritime Command Language

Rule 1: Imperative Sentences for Commands

In maritime communication, imperative sentences (commands) are essential. They start with the verb and don't use a subject.

✓ "Secure the deck!"
✗ "You should secure the deck."
Rule 2: Present Tense for Instructions

Use simple present tense when giving instructions or describing procedures on a ship.

✓ "The crew raises the sails at dawn."
✗ "The crew has been raising the sails at dawn."
Rule 3: Passive Voice in Reports

Maritime reports often use passive voice to focus on the action rather than the actor.

✓ "The cargo was loaded at 0800 hours."
✗ "They loaded the cargo at 0800 hours." (less formal)
Rule 4: Conditional Sentences for Procedures

Use conditional sentences to explain procedures and what happens if certain conditions are met.

✓ "If the weather worsens, lower the sails immediately."
✗ "When weather worsens, lower the sails immediately."
Rule 5: Phrasal Verbs in Maritime Context

Many phrasal verbs are used in maritime communication and should be learned in context.

✓ "Set out to sea" - Begin a voyage
✓ "Pull in to port" - Arrive at harbor
✓ "Cast off" - Leave from dock
